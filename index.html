Free random video chat, minus the wait

Kismet is free random video chat with the friction stripped out. There's no browsing, no swiping and no queue: you press one button, the matcher finds whoever is free, and within seconds two cameras are pointed at two faces. Done talking? Next. A different person appears, that fast, as many times as you like.

Nothing about you sticks around, either. There's no sign-up and no profile, nobody can follow you, and once a conversation ends it can't be reopened by either side. That turns out to be exactly what makes people relax: there's no history to curate and no audience to impress — just whoever is in front of you, right now.

How it works

  1. Turn your camera on

    The browser asks for permission once, and nothing is sent anywhere until you grant it. Point it at yourself with some light in front of you: a real face on screen is what makes people stay. Close the tab and the camera is off, that's the whole lifecycle.

  2. Get matched in seconds

    The matcher grabs whoever else is free right now. Want a say? Pick a language, lean toward your own country, or add interests — otherwise it's pure chance, which is half the fun.

  3. Talk — or skip to the next one

    Use your voice or the text box next to the video. Next replaces them with somebody new instantly; Stop ends everything. Once you part ways, neither of you can look the other up.

Staying safe while talking to strangers

Speed doesn't mean lawless. Kismet is adults-only and watched over by an active moderation system: every video has a report flag, pressing it is anonymous and instant, and a person who gets flagged by several different people simply stops getting matched. Bad actors don't linger here — the system is built to shed them.

Your side of the deal is the same common sense that applies to strangers everywhere: your full name, address, phone number and accounts stay yours. Anyone worth talking to doesn't need them. And the moment a conversation stops feeling right, hit Next — you don't owe a stranger an exit interview.

The rules themselves are short: be an adult, be on camera, be a human being about it. Sessions that show up without a face, push spam, or treat the camera as a dare get cut off — quickly for small things, permanently for serious ones. The aim isn't a sterile room; it's a place where pressing Start feels safe enough to do twice.

Works on everything you already own

Kismet is a website, not an app — which means the version on your phone is the same one on your laptop, updated for everyone at once, with nothing to install and nothing taking up storage. Android and iPhone use the front camera in the browser; desktops use whatever webcam is plugged in. If your device can hold a video call, it can hold this one.

The full list of requirements: a camera, a microphone, and a browser from this decade. The first time you press Start, the browser asks for camera and microphone permission — once — and the moment you close the tab, both are off. There is no background anything.

Getting better conversations

Random doesn't have to mean aimless. A few small things reliably make the difference between thirty seconds of silence and a conversation you remember:

  • Let people see you

    Face the light, not the window behind you. A visible face gets treated like a person; a silhouette gets skipped. This one setting change doubles how long people stay.

  • Open with a question

    "Where are you right now?" beats "hi" every time. Questions give the other person something to hold onto, and the first ten seconds decide whether there's a second minute.

  • Add your interests

    Two people who both typed the same interest skip the small talk entirely. It's the highest-leverage field in settings, and most people leave it empty.

  • Skip without ceremony

    Next isn't rude here — it's the etiquette. Every conversation on the site is between two people who both chose to stay, which is exactly what makes the good ones good.

Questions people ask

Does Kismet cost anything?
No. There's no subscription, no premium tier and no coins to buy. Every feature on the site is free for everyone, full stop.
Do I have to create an account?
Never. Kismet has no accounts at all — no email, no password, no profile. You allow your camera and you're in.
How are matches picked?
At random, from everyone who's online and searching at that moment. Preferences for language, country and interests tilt the odds in your favour, but it's a shuffle, not a search — the surprise is the point.
Can I use it on my phone?
Yes — it runs in the mobile browser on both Android and iPhone with the front camera. There's no app to download and nothing to update.
Why is a camera required?
Because face-to-face is the product. Matches where one side hides behind a black screen waste the other person's time, so everyone here is on camera — including you.
What happens to people who misbehave?
They get reported — one press on the flag over their video, anonymous, and they're never told who did it. Collect enough independent reports and the matcher cuts you off. Kismet is strictly for adults and actively moderated.
Is random video chat on Kismet safe?
Safer than the genre's reputation. Kismet is adults-only with active moderation around the clock, every video carries an anonymous one-press report, and people who collect reports stop being matched with anyone. And you're never stuck: Next ends any conversation instantly, no explanation owed.
Can anyone else see my video chat?
It's just the two of you. Wherever the network allows, video and messages go straight from one browser to the other rather than through a middleman, and conversations aren't recorded.
Is Kismet like Omegle?
The idea is the same — press once, land with a stranger — and that's about where it ends. Omegle closed in 2023; Kismet was built after it, with cameras required on both sides, active moderation and adults only. Same roulette, different room.
